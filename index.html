The course

What is persuasion, measured?

A cross-disciplinary course that treats persuasion as something you can estimate, not just describe: what changes minds, by how much, and how anyone would know.

The course explores the multifaceted nature of persuasion in the modern world, bridging political science, communication, journalism, marketing, and public health. It combines rich theories of communication with practical, hands-on analysis of real-world data.

Students quantify the effects of partisan media, social media, and political campaigns, and trace the historical evolution of persuasive techniques for the context to understand current trends and anticipate the next ones. The course fulfills the quantitative methods requirement for the Political Science major.

For complete course details, including call number, location, grading policies, and office hours, see the syllabus, the final source of truth for all of it.

  1. Randomized controlled trialsThe gold standard, from the 1948 streptomycin trial to today's A/B tests.
  2. Natural experimentsInstrumental variables and regression discontinuity when you cannot randomize.
  3. Causal inferenceFrom correlation to effect: what a number about persuasion actually means.
  4. Machine learning and banditsTargeting, Thompson sampling, contextual bandits: learning while deciding.
